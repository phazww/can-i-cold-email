Can I cold email a company in that country?

The answer changes at every border. Germany, Spain and Italy want consent first. France, Sweden and the US do not. Here are the rules for 33 countries, each linked to the law or the regulator.

Advertising by email without the addressee's prior express consent is an unacceptable nuisance under UWG section 7(2). For email the law does not distinguish consumers from businesses.

What you have to do

  • Get express consent before the first email
  • Existing customers only: you may email about your own similar products if you got the address from a sale, they have not objected, and every message offers an opt-out (section 7(3))

Method and limits

  • Each country was checked between 10 and 14 October 2026 against the text of the law or the regulator's own guidance where it could be opened. All 33 countries are checked that way.
  • "No consent needed" never means no rules. Every country still requires a real sender identity and a working way to opt out.
  • The country is taken from your list. If there is no country column, it is guessed from the domain ending (.de, .fr) and labelled as a guess.
  • Optional: a source column saying where each address came from (published if the company put it on its own website or in a register, vendor if a tool found or guessed it). In Canada, Australia, Japan, the Netherlands, Slovakia and Slovenia the verdict depends on this, and the checker uses it.
  • An address is treated as generic when the part before @ is a role word such as info, contact or sales, and as personal when the domain is a consumer mail provider such as gmail.com.
  • Not covered: existing customers, newsletters people signed up for, phone, SMS, LinkedIn, and the sender's own national rules.

This is a research summary, not legal advice. Rules are those of the recipient's country and cover the first unsolicited marketing email to a business recipient. Data protection law (GDPR, UK GDPR) applies separately whenever an address identifies a person.
